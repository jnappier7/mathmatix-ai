How the prep works

Taking a test is free and needs no account. A free account is what turns the score into a plan.

  1. Take a test. Timed and full-length, with no pausing, just like test day.
  2. See what you missed. Your score, your likely range, and how you did in each content area. A free account shows you exactly which questions you got wrong.
  3. Review with your tutor. Your AI tutor goes through each miss with you, teaches the skill behind it and the test-taking move that would have saved time, then gives you fresh practice on it.
  4. Re-test. A new form that leaves out the questions you’ve already answered, so the change in your score reflects what you learned, not what you remember.

Common questions

Not sure which test is yours? Start here.

Which practice test should I take?

Ninth and tenth graders: the PreACT or the PSAT, whichever your school gives. Juniors: the PSAT in the fall, then the SAT or ACT. Seniors: the SAT or ACT. If you haven’t picked between the SAT and ACT yet, take both practice tests. They’re free, and the one you score higher on is usually the one to prepare for.

Are the scores official?

No. Each score is an estimate, converted from your raw score the way the real test converts it. It’s a good read on where you stand today, not a score a college will see.

Do I need an account?

Not to take a test or see your score. A free account saves your results, shows you exactly which questions you missed, and lets your AI tutor review each one with you.

Can I use a calculator?

Yes, on every test, just like the real thing. The SAT and PSAT have Desmos and the reference sheet built in. The ACT and PreACT have a calculator built in.

Can I take it again?

Yes. Each re-test is a new form that leaves out the questions you’ve already answered, so a higher score means you learned something, not that you remembered the answers.
